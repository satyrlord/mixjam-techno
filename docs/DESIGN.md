# Design System: MixJam Techno

## Overview

**Design goal: "A clear live-set score"**

The arrangement is the main work area. Graphite surfaces separate clip names,
track numbers, and playback controls. Lime marks primary actions. Category
colors identify the same sounds in the browser and arrangement.

The owner selected this design for the classic loop sequencer. It uses imported
Syncopate branding and titles, Inter Medium interface text, flat controls,
and consistent spacing.
Every in-app window and modal uses concept 1, Folded Metal: subtly aged folded
painted metal, dark inset control bays, cream text, polished silver knobs, and
pale metal primary buttons. Fine grain, minor scratches, and restrained edge wear
suggest a well-maintained early-2000s synth. Keep the original tool palettes:
dark marine cyan for Time Stretcher, dark granite gray for Sample Editor, synth
red for Effect Studio, dark olive for Step synth, and dark indigo for the
Mastering board and its help. Utility dialogs use neutral
metal with an optional orange-yellow accent. The common orange-yellow mock color
does not replace these identities.
One shell and control family covers musical tools and utility tasks. The main
graphite workspace and Mixer's graphite and ivory strips keep their current design.
Native operating-system windows and file pickers retain their OS presentation.

RaveFold supplies artistic references only. It does not govern product
behavior; if `local-data/research/PRODUCT.md` and
`local-data/research/COMPATIBILITY.md` exist, they do.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Graphite surfaces with off-white text and restrained borders.
- Syncopate branding and titles in uppercase; Inter Medium for all other text.
- A wide arrangement with transport above and sound discovery below.
- Lime primary actions, category-colored clips, and coral recording signals.

The implementation is native Windows Rust with eframe/egui. Workspace tokens and
painting live in `src/ui/view.rs`; Mixer stays in `src/ui/mixer.rs`.
`src/ui/dialog.rs` owns every floating shell and its lifecycle.
`src/ui/controls.rs` owns reusable control rendering and interaction. Tool bodies
remain in `src/ui/stretch.rs`, `src/ui/effects.rs`, `src/ui/step_synth.rs`, and
`src/ui/sample_editor.rs`; task adapters remain in `src/ui.rs`. Application state
and behavior live in `src/application.rs`. Sizes here use portable `px` labels for
egui logical points. The sidecar records native component metadata; it does not
define browser components or application behavior.

## Colors

### Primary

Accent lime identifies Play/Pause, primary empty-state actions,
the playhead, enabled routing controls, and focus or active-state outlines.
Coral identifies recording, errors, missing sources, and selected synth notes.

### Secondary

Loop, Drum, Bass, Sequence, Sphere/Wave, Effect, Voice, and Other Sample colors
identify sounds. Tiles use a 20% category-color overlay; arrangement clips use the
full category fill with canvas-colored text. Selection adds a text-colored clip border.

**The Shared Category Rule.** Use the same category color for a sound in the
browser and arrangement. Pair color with its name or category label.

### Neutral

Canvas is the deepest structural surface. Panel supports transport, browser,
and mixer. Floating tools use their Folded Metal palette. Raised and Hover
distinguish controls and interaction
states. Text carries primary labels; Muted carries counts, metadata, and status.
Line separates surfaces. Selection and Warning retain their native widget roles.
Drag Label fills the label that follows the pointer while a sound or clip is
dragged; it has no other use.

Folded Metal palettes share dark inset bays and controls, cream lettering,
polished silver knob caps, and pale metal primary buttons. Tool chassis colors
remain distinct: Marine for Time Stretcher, Granite for Sample Editor, Red for
Effect Studio, Olive for Step synth, and Indigo for Mastering. Utility is
neutral and permits an orange-yellow accent. Use explicit names and states
alongside color; Coral continues to identify errors and destructive actions.
Palette changes must not change control geometry or behavior. Keep these
colors local to floating windows and the Mastering board; the arrangement,
browser, and Mixer retain their palettes.

Mastering's board fills the lower panel under the header row, an Indigo
Folded Metal surface with a 2-point inset. Its pedals are painted enclosures
on the Indigo board, each with its
own enamel and ink lettering, as stompboxes are. Light falls on them from the
upper left, at 135 degrees, as it does on the knob caps. Each enclosure has a
6-point rounded bevel that catches the light along its upper and left edges
and falls into shade along its lower and right ones, 4-point side walls in
shade below and to the right of the face, a faint sheen across the face
toward the light, and a soft drop shadow cast down and right. Text stays
inside the bevel. A pedal that is on shows anodised aluminium under its
enamel: fine brushed grain along the rows at one texel per screen pixel, a
sheen stretched across the grain (a bright streak toward the light, a darker
reflection, a fainter streak), and seeded hairline scratches where bare
aluminium shows. Each pedal keeps its own scratches, which stay below its
printed name. The finish darkens the enamel by at most 5% for the grain and
8% for the sheen, and ink keeps 5:1 there. A pedal that is off stays
matte. Families share hues, and the
enamel always appears with the pedal's printed name:

The Booster pedals print the original's names (EQ, Compressor, Stereo wide)
over a "Booster" line; the mastering pedals print their names over
their family. A name too long for its pedal shrinks, down to 11 points, until
it clears the lamp. The Limiter's rose sets it apart as the board's fixed last stage; it has no
drag grip or move buttons, and its header line says it always runs last.
The Input and Output units at the ends of the board are dark inset bays with
cream lettering, like the other tools' bays. The VU meter has a Mastering
Ivory face, ink scale, a red zone above 0 VU, and Mastering Red peak lamps.
The Output unit shows loudness and true-peak readouts in dark wells, a
loudness bar with a Mastering Green band at -14 LUFS, a true-peak bar with a
Mastering Red line at -1 dBTP, and a Mastering Red OVER lamp in the place of
a pedal's lamp. Gain-reduction lamps on the Bus compressor and Limiter are
Mastering Amber. These four tokens are local to the board. Each dynamics
pedal ends its dials with Key, which reads Own or a track number; the Bus
compressor's five dials take three columns, and the Limiter's three take two
rows. The FX windows' duckers end with the same Key dial. The strip below
the board, one 30-point row of chips in a dark well, repeats each pedal's
enamel in signal order, with a cream outline around the pedals in view.
Choosing a chip, or focusing any control above the strip, returns a scrolled
board to its top, where the units and the whole row show; focusing a chip
scrolls the board down to it. The whole pedal is painted: knobs sit directly
on the enamel with ink scale marks, ink labels, and an ink focus ring, and
values show in small dark windows with cream digits. The shared rotary draws
ink marks wherever a caller turns egui's `dark_mode` off for a light painted
surface; every other window keeps cream marks in dark bays. A lit pedal lamp
uses Accent lime with a soft glow; a pedal that is off keeps a dark lamp,
darkened knob caps, and a desaturated, darker enamel, and its chip in the
strip dims the same way. Ink on every enamel, lit or off, keeps at least 5:1
contrast. Patch cables carry a metal plug wherever they meet a socket or a
pedal.

Common controls use Face `#232624`, Cream `#f9f2dc`, Ink `#121412`, and pale
metal Primary `#d4cdb6`. Disabled faces use `#3d403c` with `#c9cbc0` lettering.
Palette accents identify focus, selection, and active indicators. A focus ring
that crosses the chassis needs 3:1 against it; Red's accent reaches only 2.6:1,
so its ring uses Cream. The default frame token uses Utility; tool bodies select
their named variant. Destructive buttons use Danger `#833627` with cream text.
Error text uses Coral in every window.

Mixer Metal and Mixer Ink support the ivory strips and their dark live text.
Mixer Cream supplies dark-control lettering. Mixer Face and Edge define native
value wells, buttons, and strip borders. Mixer Amber marks inverted polarity;
the named checked state remains available. The graphite surround and fine
ivory grain come from prepared material tiles. These tokens are local to Mixer.

## Typography

The native build embeds Syncopate Bold 700 for branding and titles, and
Inter Medium 500 for all other application text. Render branding and every
project, section, window, modal, and empty-state title in uppercase Latin text.
Transliterate special letters to their Latin counterparts before uppercase
layout, including Ș/Ş to S, Ț/Ţ to T, Â/Ă to A, and Î to I. Keep stored
project titles and other user data in their original case. Window IDs retain
their original identity independently of visible title formatting.

Syncopate is unchanged from the pinned Google Fonts source. Title letters use
their Latin counterparts instead of a Romanian fallback face. Inter is the
unchanged static text face from the official 4.1 release. Both families include
ASCII.
Egui's bundled proportional fonts follow the primary families for remaining
missing glyphs. Keep Syncopate's Apache 2.0 license, Inter's OFL license, and
provenance with the application; see `assets/fonts/README.md`.

Display is the MixJam name; Techno follows it in 14-point Syncopate and Muted.
Headline is the standard native heading. Section
labels identify Arrangement. Bottom Module supplies the lower panel's module
selector its 16-point Syncopate text. Title names the project. Body
serves fields and explanatory text. Body, Button, Clip, Small, and Metadata all
use Inter Medium 500. Track labels and numeric controls also use Inter. Clip labels use
the clip size, reduced to metadata size when their rectangle is below 28 points
high. Small and Metadata support compact controls and sample details.

The position readout shows elapsed minutes and seconds (`MM : SS`) in Inter
Medium at 23 points in a fixed 112 by 42-point container; its tooltip adds the
bar and beat. Changes to digit widths or song length must not move the
adjacent transport controls. Status text
uses Inter Medium at 12 points. These are local sizes within the same text family.

All floating windows use the same live uppercase Syncopate title and Inter body,
button, value, and status text. The shared shell uses 18-point titles, 13-point
body and button text, and 11-point small text. Content can use the shared compact
text roles where needed, without inventing per-tool typography. Keep labels separate
from controls and check rendered glyph bounds. Resizing never scales text.
Center value text and button captions inside their own visible faces. Native
widget minimums must fit the readout rectangle; they must not shift text below
the well. Check the visible glyph center at all three prepared display densities.

Mixer uses the shared Inter sizes without a local title. Track names use Body,
gain values use Clip, sound summaries and pan or Muted text use Small, and
rail marks and button lettering use Metadata. These sizes follow the selected
artwork density with the strip.

Numeric formatting is presentation only. Rounded display values must not change
stored drafts. Keep live content selectable where supported and independent of the
chassis bitmap.

**The Name Remains Rule.** Keep sample names visible on clips. Use one line below
38 points of clip height and up to two lines at larger heights. Truncate long names and
retain the full name in a tooltip.

## Layout

The native client starts at 1440 by 1000 logical points, with a minimum of 960 by
720. The header is 66 points high. Transport occupies 68 points above the
arrangement. A fixed 64-point tool rail sits at the right. The bottom panel
(Sounds, Mixer, Mastering, or Effects) starts at 28% of the content height, clamped to 220-290 points. Drag its
top divider to resize it. A centered grip marks the divider. The panel retains
its height for the current session, with a minimum of 180 points for Sounds and
400 points for Mixer. Opening Mixer raises a shorter panel to that minimum.
In a short window Mixer yields down to 370 points so the lane viewport keeps
80 points; its gain rail then shortens from 112 points to as little as 84.
When space permits, the maximum panel height leaves 220 points for the
arrangement plus 68 points for transport. At the minimum client height,
Mixer's minimum can reduce that arrangement reserve. Status
occupies 30 points below it. Its application version, prefixed with `v`, stays at the right inner
margin, with space reserved before status text is truncated. Use 12-point Inter
Medium and the theme's hyperlink color. Hover and keyboard focus show the link
underline. The version opens the GitHub repository in the system browser.
Sounds, Mixer, Mastering, and Effects share this panel size. Switching modules
preserves the resized height; opening Mixer, Mastering, or Effects first raises
a panel below its minimum. Mastering's minimum shows its whole 368-point board under
the header row; a short window lowers it to 370 points, the same floor as
Mixer, and the board then scrolls its chip strip below the pedals.

A single Bottom module selector occupies the fixed left slot in the lower
panel's 28-point header row. Its width is 168 points, enough for the full name
of every module, and its selected module uses 16-point bold Syncopate. The
selector keeps the same horizontal anchor and control identity for Sounds,
Mixer, Mastering, and Effects. A long selected name
truncates within this width; the popup and accessible value retain its full name.
Sound search and
filters occupy the rest of this row when Sounds is selected. With Mastering,
the row holds the 150-point preset selector, the 112-point Bypass all switch,
the board's state in 12-point text that truncates, and the Mastering help
button at the right margin. With Mixer, it holds the Export tracks… button. The
preset list puts a rule after Subtle, Revive, and Loud, before the genres; the
selector reads Choose a preset while every pedal is off at its defaults, and
Custom when changed pedals match no preset. The trim, which belongs to the
mix, affects neither. Module names appear
in this selector; the module bodies start below it. The tool rail's Mixer and
Mastering shortcuts open their module and return to Sounds when it is already
selected.

The arrangement shows occupied tracks and one drop target for a new track, while
the model retains 64 slots. A soloed track counts as occupied even without
clips, in the arrangement and the Mixer, so its Solo can be turned off.
Original track numbers remain stable. Rows are
36 points high at every window and panel size, so more room shows more rows,
and they scroll vertically. The label column is 122 points wide
and holds, from left, M, S, L, the track number, and R. A lane that Mute or
another track's Solo silences is dimmed, and its track number uses Muted. The
fixed ruler is 26 points high. Bars use 62 points multiplied by zoom. The
timeline scrolls horizontally without moving track labels.

Follow is enabled by default in the transport. It moves the horizontal viewport
when the playhead approaches an edge, including seeks and backward loop jumps.
It leaves vertical scrolling unchanged. While paused, manual horizontal scroll
stays where the user leaves it until the position or viewport geometry changes.
Switch Follow off to keep horizontal scrolling under manual control during
playback. The toggle is view state for the current session, not a project edit.

Sound search and the product, category, and sort menus sit above a horizontally
scrolling category row. Tiles wrap at the available width and scroll vertically.
This keeps category access separate from palette height. A tile's width follows
its bar count: 96 points per bar plus the 8-point gaps it spans, so a two-bar
tile covers two one-bar tiles. A sound longer than a row fills the row. The sort
menu orders the filtered sounds by library order, name, length, BPM, or
category; it is view state for the current session.

Mixer strips appear only for occupied tracks. Track and Master strips are
108 points wide at the supported prepared scales. Master stays outside the
horizontal track scroller, whose scrollbar remains visible. Track numbers,
sound summaries, dB gain values, pan descriptions, and output state stay
grouped within each strip. A track with
several distinct sounds shows their count and lists their names on hover.
Gain rails retain at least 112 logical points of travel at the minimum window
size. Narrow strips preserve visible tracks without enlarging every control.

Sample libraries constrains its floating window to the viewport and scrolls
vertically. A large expanded Folder tempos list must leave the provider
controls reachable.

All in-app tools, utility windows, and blocking dialogs use one resizable shell.
Initial dimensions follow the content; they are not fixed raster dimensions.
Drag any edge or corner to resize. Reflow or scroll the body without scaling its
controls. A body that overflows shows a solid scroll bar with a cream handle, so
hidden content is visible as such. Source names, action labels, and long feedback must remain reachable at
the minimum supported viewport. Keep a common title bar, margins, gaps, and action
hierarchy. Specialized bodies such as the note grid keep their domain layout.

The only window states are open and collapsed. Collapse leaves a complete header
at the same position, with the same title and applicable controls. Expansion
restores the previous dimensions and draft. Keep Help available while collapsed
where a Help action exists. A collapsed blocking dialog releases workspace input
and pauses its pending action; expanding restores its decision workflow. Do not
replace a paused request when another file action is requested.

Fixed raster detail fades from opaque texture to transparency over the matching
solid chassis color. Resizing grows the solid regions instead of stretching bitmap
edges or repeating a visible texture seam. Prepare only 100%, 150%, and 200% PNG variants;
select the nearest and paint one texel per physical screen pixel. Keep labels,
control values, indicators, and interaction states live.

Painted backgrounds use restrained, static monochromatic Gaussian grain. Its
mean is zero and its standard deviation is about 1.5 RGB levels. Apply the same
offset to all channels to retain each module's color. Align the prepared grain
to physical pixels and fade it before the perimeter. Do not animate the noise or
add grain over labels and control values.

This is a resizable Windows desktop surface. No browser, mobile, touch, or
high-DPI acceptance is implied by these logical dimensions. Native capture cases
are defined by `tools/verify-ui.ps1`; execution results belong in evidence reports.

## Elevation & Depth

Flat color layers and thin borders separate the graphite work area. Folded Metal
windows add folded painted-metal edges, dark inset control bays, recessed values, fine
grain, and sparse hairline scratches. Use no decorative screws or fasteners.
The folded lip, texture, and light wear provide the hardware character. Do not
add heavy damage, oversized bevels, or ornamental hardware. Mixer retains its existing prepared material and faders.

Mastering's pedals are the one lifted layer in the lower panel. Each stands
off the Indigo board on a soft drop shadow cast down and right, with a
6-point bevel and 4-point side walls, all lit from the upper left (see
Colors). Floating windows share one window shadow. Nothing else casts one.

Shared control rendering supplies hover, keyboard focus, pressed, selected, and
disabled states. Keep state changes immediate and visible; color alone does not
communicate state. The application has no motion tokens.

## Shapes

Clips use the smallest corner radius. Controls and sound tiles share the control
radius; transport has a slightly larger radius. Floating windows use the window
radius. Resting native widgets use one-point borders. Selected clips use a
two-point text-colored border; active rail actions use a one-point accent outline.
Transport keyboard focus uses a two-point inset outline: Canvas on the bright
Play/Pause button and Accent on secondary controls. Header actions, category
buttons, and empty-state actions use the same outline. Undo and Redo dim and
disable when no step is available. An engaged Loop keeps a
one-point Accent outline. Window edges, title bars, the lanes, and the bar ruler
are pointer surfaces and take no Tab stop.
Folded Metal windows share rounded chassis edges and the same control geometry.
The painted chassis uses 8-point corners; the 10-point window radius applies to
native popups and menus.
Paint the perimeter and bevel as continuous antialiased native geometry. Fade
raster material before the outer outline and at strip ends so joints stay smooth.
Prepare knob edges at 8x resolution, then reduce once with premultiplied-alpha
Lanczos filtering for each 100%, 150%, and 200% variant.
Live text and header symbols stay independent of the material artwork.

## Components

### Actions and transport

Workspace primary action buttons use Accent with Canvas text. Secondary buttons use Raised
with Text. Undo and Redo use transparent PNG arrow icons from the shared artwork
cache, with action names in tooltips and accessibility labels.
Help uses its own ringed question-mark PNG with Help as its accessibility
label. Tooltips name each action with its window or shortcut; accessible names
stay short.
Export WAV is available through the Export icon in the right-hand tool rail.

### Sounds and clips

Sound tiles show the name above the category label and bar count. A tile too
narrow for both keeps the bar count. A 20% overlay
of the category color tints the whole tile; there is no category dot.
Hover or selection uses Hover; selection or keyboard focus adds a one-point
category-colored border. The category and bar-count line uses Text at 80%.
Unselected category buttons use Panel with Muted text; the selected one uses
Hover, Text, and a one-point Muted border.
One tooltip groups the full name, product, category, code, sample IDs, source
tempo, nominal bars, actual audio duration, frames, rate, channels, and WAV path
when available. Nominal bars and audio duration remain separate facts.
Arrangement clips use flat category fills and names. They do not display a
waveform. A selected clip can show a gain line; it is a control indicator.
A clip whose source is missing uses Raised with a one-point Coral outline and
a Text-colored name that begins with Missing. While a sound or clip is dragged
over the lanes, clip ghosts at 60% opacity show the destination bar and track.
Group destinations on tracks without a visible row have no ghost. Off the
lanes, a dragged sound shows a 150 by 30-point Drag
Label with its name in Canvas text. A palette sound armed for click placement
shows a tinted outline where it will land. Choosing a clip or pressing Esc
ends click placement, so a click on an empty lane then only clears the
selection. Enter on a focused sound tile places
it on the recording track at the playhead. Lane M/S/L/R letters gain a short
bar when their switch is on. The lanes are one Tab stop with an Accent outline:
arrow keys choose a clip, Alt+arrows move the selection by a bar or a track,
Ctrl+Alt+arrows by a sixteenth, Enter previews, Delete removes, and Shift+F10
opens the selected clip's menu under the clip. Delete and Shift+F10 also work
when no control has focus. Ctrl+A selects every clip and Ctrl+D copies
the selection to the bars that follow. A right-click on a clip offers Preview,
Duplicate, Delete, and, for a missing sound, Resolve manually.

A clip is edited on the clip itself, as in other DAWs. Its side edges trim it:
the right edge sets its length, and the left edge moves its start and trim
together, so the sound stays where it plays. Edges snap to bars, or to
sixteenths with Ctrl. Small dark squares on the top edge set the fade in and
fade out; they snap to sixteenths, or to ticks with Ctrl. A small handle on
the gain line, in the middle of the clip's visible part, sets the gain: each
4 points up or down is 1 dB, or 0.1 dB with Ctrl, from +6 dB to silence. A
label at the pointer shows the value, and the release makes one undo step.
Where the gain handle covers another handle, the visible gain handle receives
the input. The rest of the clip still moves it; Shift, Alt, or the right button
copies it. The right-click menu also holds the exact values: gain in dB like
the Mixer, trim start in
frames, length, fades with their curves, and pitch in semitones and cents.
The arrangement has no clip strip under the lanes. Clips with available audio
show their name without a hover tooltip. Missing clips keep their metadata and
resolution guidance in a tooltip.

### Mixer and tool windows

Mixer uses compact ivory strips against a graphite material surround. Track
identity and gain lead the hierarchy. L/R routing uses small named toggle
buttons with live enabled indicators. Mute and Solo sit in their own row
above polarity and L/R, after a 26 by 24-point EQ button above polarity that
fills amber while the track's EQ is on. The status line under pan names why a track is
silent: Muted, then No output (both L and R off), then Solo elsewhere.
Gain runs from
negative infinity (silence) to +12 dB with 0 dB at unity. Negative dB attenuates
the signal. Values round to one decimal place at rest; the active entry field
shows full precision. Focus without an edit must not change saved amplitude
or audio. Relative increases must leave silence and raise very quiet positive
gains. A saved gain below -96 dB must not become silent when increased.
Master uses the same dB presentation and remains outside the scroller. In
place of pan and routing it shows Main output and L + R, and under them an
80 by 24-point Bypass button that fills amber while the master pedalboard is
bypassed. A bypassed Mastering board draws at 45% opacity and stays usable.

The channel EQ window uses the neutral Utility palette. A power switch and
Reset share its top row; the response curve, from 20 Hz to 20 kHz and ±24 dB
on a dark inset with grid lines at 100 Hz, 1 kHz, 10 kHz, and 12 dB steps,
follows; five columns below name the bands, Low cut, Low shelf, Mid peak, High
shelf, and High cut, with their Mastering dials stacked. The curve uses the
palette's print color while the EQ is on and faint cream while it is off;
the dials are disabled while it is off. Below the EQ, the ducker's power
switch starts a row of five dials, Key (a track number), Thresh, Depth,
Attack, and Release, disabled while the ducker is off. The Key dial steps
over the window's own track. The strip's EQ button lights while the EQ or
the ducker is on.

The rail uses a piecewise dB taper. From bottom to top, its normalized knots
are silence at 0, -60 dB at 0.08, -36 dB at 0.16, -24 dB at 0.28, -12 dB at
0.46, unity at 0.70, and +12 dB at 1. The bottom segment maps linear amplitude
to exact zero; the remaining segments interpolate in dB. Thus 72% of travel
serves -24 through +12 dB. The scale marks useful values without spreading
very quiet settings over most of the rail. Its labels are +12, 0, -12, -24,
-48, and negative infinity; 0 sits left of the rail beside the Unity button. These knots record the current
implementation, not a universal DAW taper.

The small Unity button sits at the 0 dB gain mark. The small Center button
resets pan at its rail midpoint. A separate slashed-circle button reverses
both channels' polarity. Amber means inverted and its checked state is named.
Polarity stays independent of gain and pan, even at silence; Unity leaves it
unchanged. Pan retains a readable left/center/right value. Every control has
a track-specific accessible name; Master has its own gain labels. The header
uses the shared Bottom module selector. Control-specific tooltips and the Help
window explain silence, unity, attenuation, and independent polarity.

`src/ui/mixer.rs` paints prepared ivory and graphite tiles and a separate
24 by 32-point fader cap. Nine PNGs cover 100%, 150%, and 200% density. The tiny
cap is first reduced to a 48 by 64-pixel canonical grid before preparing the
lower densities, preserving broad ridges and its ivory indicator stripe. All
text, values, rails, ticks, reset buttons, polarity, routing, focus, and hover
remain native. Select the nearest density and paint one texel per physical
pixel with aligned bounds; tile materials without runtime texture resizing.
See `assets/mixer/README.md` for exact prompts, source hashes, and reproduction.
Native mixer buttons use Mixer Face at rest and shared Raised on hover;
keyboard focus changes their one-point border to Mixer Amber and adds a
two-point Mixer Ink ring, because amber alone is illegible on ivory. The gain
rail, pan rail, and value field show the same ink ring or a cream edge. Each 28 by
24-point M/S/L/R button pairs its letter with a 12 by 2-point indicator bar.
Arrangement and mixer M/S/L/R labels use Accent when on and Muted when off.
Both expose the same checked state and a track-specific on/off tooltip. Lane
controls show keyboard focus. A lane edit requests a repaint so the already
drawn mixer refreshes even if the pointer stops. Pan remains independent of L/R.
The sample-conflict modal confirms only through Keep choices, because
confirmation is an undoable project change; Esc hides it without an edit and
the status bar reopens it.
The idle status reads Ready. Errors and application notices remain visible.
The empty mixer has one instruction for adding a sound to the arrangement.
All tool windows and utility dialogs use the same Folded Metal shell. The
frontend submits edits to the application layer; visual changes preserve those
commands.

## Design rules

### Use

- Preserve graphite surfaces, Syncopate branding and titles, Inter Medium
  interface text, and lime primary actions.
- Use the same category and sample names across views.
- Keep track numbers stable and give actions accessible names.
- Check native layouts at the supported minimum size after changes.

### Accepted exceptions

The owner confirmed these on 2026-10-12 after an audit. Do not report them as
defects.

- Mixer Unity, Center, pan rail, and gain value stay below 24 points high. The
  compact strip is intended.
- Step synth note cells stay 14 points high so that 19 of 25 pitch rows show at
  once. Arrow keys reach every cell.
- The tool rail stays icon-only. Tooltips name each action, its window, and its
  shortcut.
- The Sounds panel keeps both the category menu and the category row. The menu
  lists the library's own categories, including Uncategorized.
- The product notes leave Platform unclassified. No value of the design skill
  describes a Windows desktop application.

### Avoid

- Keep Folded Metal styling on in-app floating windows and modals. Preserve the
  main graphite workspace and Mixer. Do not add repeated waveform traces.
- Do not use color as the only way to identify a sound or action.
- Do not put application rules in the visual layer.
- Do not use a screenshot as evidence for audio parity, device behavior, or
  assistive technology support.
